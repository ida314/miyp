Dana — Free to use, freely supported

The teachings of the Buddha were given freely. This tool follows that tradition.

The Dana Model

Dana (Pāli: dāna) is the practice of generosity — one of the foundational virtues in Buddhist ethics. For 2,500 years, the dhamma has been transmitted without price. Teachers taught freely; communities supported teachers through voluntary offerings. No one was turned away from the teachings because they could not pay.

Monk in Your Pocket is free to use for everyone, with no account required and no paywall. Rate limits exist only to prevent abuse, not to meter access. If the tool has been useful to you, you are warmly invited — but never obligated — to offer dana.

Conversations are never sold, used for advertising, or shared with third parties. There are no premium tiers. Guidance is not withheld.

When the monthly math is known, it will be published here. There is no opacity in this model — if you give, you deserve to know where it goes.

The Principles Behind This Choice

  • ☸ The teachings are not for sale. Paywalling guidance on suffering and its end would contradict the very purpose of the project.
  • ☸ No advertising. The attention economy runs on craving and distraction — the opposite of what the dhamma cultivates. There will never be ads here.
  • ☸ No dark patterns. No guilt, no countdown timers, no "you have 3 free responses left." Dana is freely given or not given at all.
  • ☸ Surplus is generosity, not profit. Right livelihood means covering what's needed, then letting the rest flow outward.
"If beings knew, as I know, the results of giving and sharing, they would not eat without having given, nor would the stain of miserliness overcome their minds." — The Buddha, Itivuttaka 26
