About Monk in Your Pocket

What it is, how it was built, and what it is not.

The Name

The name isn't a claim — it's an aspiration about access.

For most of human history, Buddhist teachings were available only to those who lived near a monastery, had a teacher, or belonged to a community. Most people didn't. "Monk in Your Pocket" means the teachings themselves are now close at hand, whenever you need them — not a monk, not a therapist, not a replacement for practice or community. Just 2,500 years of practical wisdom, organized, searchable, and expressed in plain language.

The playfulness in the name is intentional. This tool is approachable, not authoritative. It is a starting point, not a destination.

The Idea

Most AI chatbots that touch on Buddhism fall into one of two failure modes: they produce generic, vaguely spiritual-sounding text that sounds like Buddhism without being grounded in it, or they point you to raw canonical passages that are often too dialogic, repetitive, or monastically-oriented to be immediately useful.

Monk in Your Pocket is built on a different bet: that the right unit of retrieval isn't a sutta chapter, but a structured advice unit — a normalized, practice-oriented summary of what a specific passage actually teaches about a specific human problem. Each unit captures the problem it addresses, the Buddhist diagnostic interpretation, the key teaching, and concrete steps a modern lay practitioner can take. Responses are built from these units, not from raw text, and every response cites the original source so you can verify and go deeper.

The Corpus

Monk in Your Pocket draws on the early Buddhist texts of the Pāli Canon, specifically the Bhikkhu Sujato translations available on SuttaCentral. The current corpus covers:

Collection Full name Focus
AN Aṅguttara Nikāya Practical, enumerated teachings for lay life
MN Majjhima Nikāya The middle-length discourses; core doctrine and practice
SN Saṁyutta Nikāya (selected) Thematically grouped suttas on hindrances, factors of awakening, and mindfulness

Each sutta is processed through an extraction pipeline that identifies whether it contains practical teaching for laypeople, extracts structured advice units, and validates them before they enter the retrieval index. Units that are low-confidence or ambiguous are flagged for human review.

How Retrieval Works

When you submit a message, the system runs seven steps before generating a response:

  1. Safety check — crisis keywords are detected immediately, before any LLM call
  2. Query parsing — your message is classified by problem type, emotion, and Buddhist diagnostic label
  3. Classification — labels are validated against the ontology; crisis indicators are checked again
  4. Retrieval — advice units are matched by label and semantic similarity over embeddings
  5. Reranking — candidates are scored by label match, confidence, and review status
  6. Planning — the best sources and teaching points are selected and structured
  7. Writing — a grounded response is generated in plain language with citations
Questions like "what are you?" or "hello" are detected early and answered directly without running the retrieval pipeline. This keeps responses appropriate to context.

What This Is Not

  • A licensed therapist, counselor, or crisis service. If you are in crisis, please contact the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988).
  • A replacement for a teacher, a sangha, or personal practice. No AI can substitute for relationship and direct transmission.
  • Comprehensive of all Buddhist traditions. This focuses exclusively on early Pāli texts and makes no claim about Mahāyāna, Vajrayāna, or Zen traditions.
  • Infallible. LLM extraction of advice units can misinterpret or over-generalize. All units are confidence-scored; low-confidence units are flagged.
  • A meditation app. There are no timers, guided sessions, or streaks here.

The Translations

All source texts use the Bhikkhu Sujato translations via SuttaCentral, which are made freely available under a Creative Commons license. Sujato's translations are chosen for their contemporary language and accessibility.
