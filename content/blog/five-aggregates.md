---
title: The Five Aggregates and "This Is Not Myself"
date: 2026-04-20
excerpt: A look at the Buddhist framework that categorizes all aspects of human experience — and what it reveals about the nature of the self.
---

The five aggregates are a Buddhist concept that categorizes all the ways we exist as a person. They are:

- **Form:** Your body's physical existence and the material world around you
- **Feeling:** Sensations that arise from contact with the world — these can be pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral
- **Perception:** The mental process of recognizing and labeling what we encounter
- **Mental Formations:** Volitional activities, intentions, habits, and emotional responses
- **Consciousness:** The basic awareness that arises through each of the six senses

These five aggregates make up the human experience. Everything that makes you who you are falls within them. Your habits and tendencies fall under mental formations. Your raw emotions fall under feeling. Your physical body falls under form.

However, when we inspect them more closely, we find that none of these five factors are stable — they are constantly changing moment to moment. This is the Buddhist argument for the absence of a fixed self: if all the factors that make up a person are constantly changing, then whatever we call our "self" — however it appears within these five aggregates — is also constantly changing. And since we tend to define the self as something fixed and immutable, there is no such self, because what we take to be our self is in constant flux. This is the intellectual path to understanding non-self (*anattā*).

Since these five aggregates make up who you are but are not a fixed "self," we can begin to treat them as objects we observe rather than things we are. For example, we can stop thinking of feelings — whether pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral — as part of ourselves, and instead treat them as objects in our immediate experience, external to what we consider "self."
