---
title: Buddhist Temple in Vietnam
date: 2026-02-05
excerpt: Walking meditation inside a packed Hanoi temple during Chinese New Year — finding stillness in the middle of chaos.
---

Over Chinese New Year break, amid the hustle and bustle of Hanoi — both in the crowds and in our travel itinerary — I visited a Buddhist temple. This was the tail end of a one-week trip, during which we had just come from Đà Nẵng, a stretch that hadn't involved much mindfulness practice. So when I reached Hanoi, I had the opportunity to visit a temple and decompress.

The temple sat beside a lake, separated from a bustling street by a narrow bridge that connected the sidewalk to the entrance. The bridge was packed with tourists and worshippers alike. I cut through the crowd, glancing up once to see a thin golden spire inscribed with small statues of Buddhist monks along its exterior. The further I went inside, the more packed the temple became — but I didn't let this bother me.

Before entering, I had set the intention to stay mindful throughout my visit and to practice walking meditation while moving through the temple. Walking meditation works the same way as sitting meditation, but instead of using the breath as an anchor, I used the sensation of my feet making contact with the ground. So I continued in this way as I moved through the temple.

During this practice, I noticed frustration at the crowd rising in my mind. Just as I would during sitting meditation, I recognized it as a thought and returned to the sensation of my feet, with compassion. I continued until I found a quiet corner in a small interior courtyard and sat down. For about forty minutes, I sat watching people as they passed. Some were tourists like me, encountering an unfamiliar Buddhist temple with fresh eyes. Others were local Vietnamese, visiting with their families and paying their respects.

As I left, what I carried with me was a sense of grounding amid the chaos of the Chinese New Year break.
