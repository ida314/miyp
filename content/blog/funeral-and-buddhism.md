---
title: Funeral and a Personal Connection to Buddhism
date: 2026-03-15
excerpt: My grandfather's passing, a traditional Japanese funeral, and how months of Buddhist practice changed what that experience meant to me.
---

Recently, my grandfather on my mother's side passed away. He was Japanese and, interestingly, a Christian — but his funeral procession followed the traditional Japanese style, which is heavily influenced by Buddhism and Shinto. The proceedings included a monk, and thirty minutes to an hour of chanting, bell-ringing, and burning incense.

While I am Japanese, I have never lived in Japan. Sometimes I feel in touch with my culture, but at times — as at the funeral — many of the practices felt foreign to me. However, this happened in the midst of my Buddhism project, and I had already done months of practice and research into what Buddhism means and how it is practiced. I found that this background helped me stay grounded during the proceedings, making the experience feel less foreign and more familiar.

During the monk's chanting, I approached it the same way I had been approaching meditation — but instead of using the breath as a focus, I used the sounds the monk was producing as my anchor. His chants, Buddhist readings, rhythmic tapping, clapping, and the sounds of handling various Buddhist artifacts became the object of attention. Through this, my mind became more present — more aligned with what Buddhism actually teaches about attention and awareness. Since the procession was a Buddhist one, I believe this allowed me to feel more connected to the funeral itself, and indirectly more connected to my grandfather in that moment of grief. It also enabled me to connect more with my Japanese side of the family — understanding the practice informed how I carried myself during the ceremony.

The experience also revealed how many connections I already had with Buddhism. When I first took an interest in Buddhism at the start of this semester, I was drawn to it purely for psychological reasons — gaining clarity, developing mindfulness, reducing suffering. But as I researched and practiced more, I began to realize how many parallels Buddhism already had in my life that I hadn't noticed. I have been visiting Buddhist temples in Japan since I was a baby. My mother and grandmother both follow, albeit informally, Japanese Zen Buddhism. On my Japanese side of the family, Buddhist tradition has been quietly present my entire life.
