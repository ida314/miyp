---
title: Observation vs Suppression
date: 2026-04-01
excerpt: The shift that made the biggest difference in my practice — learning to observe thoughts and feelings rather than fight them.
---

One thing that separates my current practice from past attempts to meditate is the concept of observing feelings and thoughts rather than suppressing them.

Previously, I approached meditation through a Western lens, trying to maximize the amount of time I was able to stay concentrated on my breath. So when my mind inevitably began to wander, I met those thoughts and emotions with frustration, trying to suppress them as distractions. This led to a persistent sense of not being "good enough" at meditation.

The central change I made was to stop caring about being "good" at meditation, and to question what "good" even meant in that context. I stopped thinking of meditation as trying to set a new personal record for how long I could concentrate on my breath. Instead, I began observing the breath while allowing thoughts and emotions to arise, meeting them with compassion — how lucky I am to have the ability to think and feel — and then gently returning to the breath.

This small shift in my approach made a world of difference. I stopped getting frustrated at getting distracted, and at not being able to sustain focus for long stretches. Paradoxically, this actually improved my ability to concentrate on the breath. When I stopped fighting distraction, it lost most of its power.
